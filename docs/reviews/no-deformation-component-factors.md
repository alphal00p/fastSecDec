# Componentwise exceptional-factor extraction

The caller-stepped factor frontier extracts existing exceptional Cartier factors
on checked smooth algebraic charts. It uses native regular-component splitting,
principal-open restrictions, Cartier division and quotient-ring reduction. It
does not choose general resolution centers or certify a real integration atlas.

Each original generator retains its own quotient through every restriction and
division. Exact recombination is checked after each operation; temporary native
ideal normalization cannot silently change generator association. A disconnected
boundary can have different powers on different components. Both sides of every
split remain in the coverage graph, including explicit empty-open evidence.
Ambient zero-ideal components are geometric outcomes, never zero integrals.

Before accepting a leaf, the producer absorbs factors that have become units
and recomputes componentwise maximality for the final residual ideal. The
residual need not be nonzero at every point: the assertion is that it is not
identically zero on any remaining boundary component. Old-divisor history and
parameter roles remain attached to the actual source owner.

The algorithm is independent of polynomial degree. Focused controls include a
degree-five boundary with powers zero, two and three, and an implicit degree-five
presentation with a checked derivative guard. Other controls cover multiple
generators, mixed components, unit boundaries, reordered work, resource stops
and retries. Independent root and runtime-agent reviews accept the local
mathematics, native reuse and provenance boundaries.

Limits bound frontier nodes, depth, extracted powers and checker work. An
incomplete step preserves the accepted state and its failure evidence; it does
not yield a completed factorization. Native inner-CAS allocations still require
caller-owned process limits. This in-memory frontier does not yet provide a
durable checkpoint or a global BM termination invariant.
