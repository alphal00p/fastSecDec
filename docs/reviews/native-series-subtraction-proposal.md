# Native series-first subtraction experiment

This is a bounded, test-only generation experiment. Production subtraction and Laurent expansion remain unchanged. The triggering evidence is `native-laurent-depth-probe.md`: one on-shell triple-box representative has a 5.7 MB opaque epsilon template and a 256 MB finite coefficient. Native relative-depth expansion reduces its series time from 149 s to 37 s, but does not reduce the output size. Native denominator projection also times out on the on-shell input, so reducing the parameter dimension alone does not resolve the bottleneck.

## Native ownership and scope

The active reference direct builder first obtains regular epsilon coefficients, differentiates those coefficients at coordinate faces, assembles formal endpoint expressions, and finally substitutes the coefficient bodies into a single explicit evaluator. Its relevant paths are `src/integrand.py` `_g_coefficients_by_symbolic_diff`, `_two_stage_derivative_fused_components`, and `build_explicit_sector_formula`. This intermediate symbolic ordering does not require its separate U/F evaluator path.

Symbolica already supplies the necessary algebra: `Series<AtomField>::map_coeff` in `src/poly/series.rs:717` preserves the native truncation metadata while mapping coefficient Atoms; native series addition and multiplication own convolution and precision propagation. Native `Atom::series` handles leading cancellations, poles, Gamma callbacks, and essential-singularity errors. `AtomField` uses ordinary Atom arithmetic without an implicit expansion pass. No series dictionary, convolution, Gamma expansion, derivative engine, or generic pole estimator is introduced.

The experiment expands each mapped regular factor in epsilon before coordinate subtraction. Coordinate derivatives and endpoint replacements act coefficientwise through `map_coeff`. Endpoint denominator inverses, coordinate powers, and prefactors are themselves native series. Taylor and optional IBP branch selection use the existing exact affine endpoint-power admission and limits. Identical prefactors are grouped before the final native series product, as in production.

The private mapped-input prototype inherits production admission; it does not claim these operations commute for arbitrary Atoms. In particular a moving face singularity such as `1/(x+epsilon)` is outside the premise. The public parametric IR rejects regulator-dependent singularity polynomials, polynomial-role factors have nonnegative integer powers, and mapped singular residual faces are certified. These existing constraints supply joint face regularity for the intended graph workload; no extra generic algebra validator is introduced.

## Precision and zero contract

All input expansions initially use one positive native relative width. Native series operations compose the entire requested vector, including boundary pieces. The result is usable only if its native absolute remainder bound is strictly greater than the requested maximum Laurent order. Otherwise the experiment increases the input width by the observed bound deficit, recomputes, and checks the final bound again. It never assumes the bound improves by that amount. Explicit iteration and width limits prevent an unbounded retry. The successful bound and every attempted width/time are reported.

A truncated zero is not an identically zero function. Known-zero Taylor coefficients and remainders are therefore retained with their native remainder metadata until the final coverage check. `map_coeff(|_| 0)` preserves that absolute bound; native `Series::zero`, which resets the shift, is not used to initialize Taylor sums. Native coefficient extraction rejects fractional Laurent orders and coefficients still containing epsilon.

The commutation argument is limited to already admitted regular mapped factors: coordinate differentiation and substitution at a resolved regular face preserve the formal epsilon remainder order; subtraction is linear; multiplication by every meromorphic endpoint factor is performed by native series arithmetic. No separate inferred pole budget is needed. Inputs with epsilon-independent singular powers take an explicit whole-density production-subtraction fallback, followed by native bounded series. A finite zero series cannot certify all-order vanishing of an unregulated endpoint, and the prototype can retain mathematically zero branches that production prunes by an exact derivative-independence proof. The fallback avoids assuming correspondence between those branches. Its route is recorded per attempt and does not cover the targeted regulated graph workload.

## Controls and bounded actual workload

Small controls compare complete Laurent vectors against production for Taylor and IBP: single/double endpoint poles, scaled Gamma poles, prefactor-zero cancellation, negative maximum orders, fractional coordinate exponents, complex weights, multiaxis polynomials, exact cancellations, and empty results. Independent analytic coefficients check the double pole `1/(2 epsilon^2)` and a degree-10000 compact regular factor. A regulated axis whose polynomial remainder is exactly pruned, followed by an unregulated singular axis, specifically checks the fallback and its analytic pole/finite coefficients. Fractional epsilon powers, essential singularities, and unregulated endpoints must retain typed errors. Native normalization for exact control comparisons is size-bounded and never applied to the large workload solely to force canonical equality.

A test-only capture hook exports original mapped regular factors, powers, prefactors, coordinate symbols, and representative association before subtraction. Earlier Laurent calls may be skipped only inside the explicit capture guard, which always cancels generation and cannot return an incomplete integral. The actual comparison is representative index 38/display ordinal 39 of the preserved on-shell input, source chart 46 and multiplicity 2. Its new outputs will be compared against all six previously exported orders, with exact Atom equality where representations agree. Otherwise representation differences are recorded and matched native high-precision values are compared at the same points; large blanket expansions are prohibited. Setup, series composition, output size, and any numeric comparison are timed separately.

No default change or performance claim follows from the prototype. The combined main-library and native-family targets typechecked. The first five-control execution passed four controls; the compact-polynomial control's exact Atom comparison encountered equivalent grouping of `-(1+10000*x)` and `-1-10000*x`. The raw initial log is retained as `output/series-first-controls-initial.log`. The test now keeps the shared high-degree body opaque while checking the small identity, in addition to the exact pole, full finite coefficient, and complete order list. All five controls then passed in 0.05 s (`output/series-first-controls.log`); the three existing capture/depth/error controls also passed after the capture-hook change (`output/series-first-capture-controls.log`). The completed bounded representative replay is recorded below; its separate full-vector comparison remains pending.

## Source autopsy and next compactness boundary

The unsuccessful replay still performs coordinate derivatives on full native coefficient bodies and copies those bodies into each endpoint branch. Its 384 pieces then undergo native series products with coordinate weights and finally the common prefactor. Native arithmetic is correct, but changing the order alone does not supply a shared representation of those repeated bodies.

The active reference direct route supplies two additional representation boundaries:

* `src/integrand.py:6762`, `_g_coefficients_by_symbolic_diff`, constructs regular epsilon coefficients from a fixed-power prefactor and **one combined logarithm**. The logarithm combines U/F exponent slopes and nonsingular-coordinate monomial slopes (`_regular_monomial_base_log_expr`, line 6610). It differentiates local Taylor polynomials whose coefficients are source derivative slots. The reference's explicit log-power/factorial formula and Python series dictionaries are not proposed for copying; native Series/exp can own equivalent algebra if this ordering is separately justified and probed.
* `_two_stage_derivative_fused_components`, line 6968, groups requested derivatives by endpoint/zero-coordinate face. `grouped_pairs`/`g_expr_cache` retain only requested `(derivative multiindex, epsilon order)` pairs. The topology-independent endpoint assembler consumes formal coefficient symbols. The direct residual branch computes native residual polynomial derivatives at the requested faces before assembling the regular template. At lines 7245 onward, regular coefficient bodies replace formal assembler slots. `build_explicit_sector_formula`, line 7827, then substitutes source derivative bodies and creates **one explicit multioutput evaluator**. Reusing this symbolic organization does not require the rejected separate U/F runtime evaluator.

The next research target is therefore opacity that preserves coordinate dependence. Native Symbolica already represents derivatives of unknown functions as `Symbol::DERIVATIVE(depths..., function, arguments...)` (`src/derivative.rs:60–219`) and owns repeated differentiation and the chain rule. Replacing a regular coefficient or an admitted epsilon-independent residual subexpression by a function of its actual coordinate arguments can keep the body out of endpoint differentiation, while a coordinate-independent placeholder cannot do so correctly.

A bounded follow-up may retain those formal functions through native series/Taylor composition, discover the native derivative calls, and supply their bodies once through native FunctionMap definitions. Each source derivative must still be computed by Symbolica, with an exact cache key containing the source body, derivative multiindex and relevant coordinate binding. Only exact native zero derivatives may be eliminated. One native evaluator would own all outputs and definitions; there is no new numerical evaluator or hand differentiation/series arithmetic. A second option is delayed literal restoration for comparison, but restoring large bodies into every output may recreate the measured storage problem.

This is a proposal, not an implemented optimization. The source/library API audit and full-vector validation must decide the exact seam. Any evaluator experiment must recheck available Symbolica/SymJIT versions, include every definition body in size and identity accounting, preserve MPFR/complex/weighted paths and cold serialization, and compare explicit inlining policies without conflating their translation settings. Its first objective is completing the currently blocked on-shell generation. Whole-project tuning of lattice convergence or maximum/mean sample cost comes only after the required capability coverage; no per-sample performance improvement is inferred from a smaller formal expression.
